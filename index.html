§ 01 Manifesto three paragraphs  /  one principle

“The best code, like the cleanest line call, is the one that ends an argument before it starts.”

I'm a software engineer at INNOV8 Fusion, where I build full-stack HRM and SCM platforms on .NET Core, Angular and SQL Server. Performance, automation, and the kind of user experience that doesn't make people sigh — those are the three things I'm paid to fight for, and the three I actually care about.

Off the keyboard I'm a National Umpire with Sri Lanka Badminton. It sounds unrelated. It isn't. Calling a shot in or out in front of a crowd is the same muscle as shipping a refactor on a Friday: read the signal, trust the judgement, commit without flinching, accept the review.

Whichever side of the net I'm on, the work is the same — make a precise call, keep the game moving, never make it about you.

Why it mattersDecision-making under live pressure

On court my job is to see what happened, not what should have happened. The shuttle either crossed the line or it didn't. The call has to come out fast, clean, and audible — and then the match keeps moving.

That's the discipline I carry into engineering. Code reviews, production incidents, contested design choices — all of them get easier when you've practised calling a line in front of a packed gym.

“In or out — but say it.”
